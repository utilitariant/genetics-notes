---
title: lac Operon
---

A highly conserved DNA sequence that contains both coding and regulatory genes, all related to the metabolism of lactose.
___
## Cis-Regulatory Elements:
Nucleotide sequence elements that can only regulate DNA that is Upstream or downstream from it on the same nucleic acid chain.

- ### Promoter Sequence
	non-coding DNA sequence upstream from the operon...     
	recognized by [[RNA polymerase]] which binds to the sequence in order to initiate expression of the operon downstream. <br></br>
- ### Operator Sequence
	#### &nbsp; $\large{lacO}$  
	non-coding DNA sequence upstream from the operon, that allows for its inducible expression
	<br></br>
	- #### $\large{[O^c]}$ = Constitutive operator mutant
		Mutant operator sequence that looses affinity for the Repressor protein... Meaning that it can no longer be repressed... this results in the downstream Operon being expressed continually (constitutive expression), regardless of the presence of other Regulatory elements.
___
## Trans-Regulatory Elements:
Protein Regulatory elements that can act on any DNA in the cell.

- ### Repressor Protein Gene: 
	#### &nbsp; $\large{lacI}$
	When functional, the produced gene will bind to the 
	Protein contains allosteric site.
	Activity controlled by presence of lactose in nucleus.
	When present, Allolactose molecule acting as an inducer, that binds to the repressor proteins allosteric site and changes its shape in a way that prevents it from binding to the operator
	$lacI$ gene expresses a [[TREs|trans-acting]] repressor protein. the $lacI$ gene is separate from the operon and is unregulated.  
	- $\large{[I^-]}$
	  ### Mutant Repressor Protein 
		$\large{[I^-]}$    
		mutant lac repressor proteins shape is altered, in a way that it can no longer bind to the operator.
		lac genes are expressed in the absence and the presence of inducer (constitutive expression)
	
	- ### Mutant Superrepressor Protein 
		$\large{[I^s]}$    
		$\large s=$ <u>superrepressor</u>     
		the superrepressor can bind to the operator, however it lacks the ability to bind to the inducer
		- lac genes are repressed in the absence and presence of inducer
		     
		#### Mutation effects.
		allosteric site on protein changes shape... 
		the inducer (allolactose) can no longer bind...
		$lac$ operon expression silenced...
		Lactose is not able to be metabolized.
	
## Gene Coding Constituents:
--- 
- $\large lacZ$ Gene   
	Codes for [[beta-galactosidase|β-galactosidase]],   
		An enzyme that Hydrolyzes lactose into galactose and glucose.
- $\large lacY$ Gene  
	Codes for [[Lac permease]],  
		A Membrane protein that pumps lactose into a cell.
- $\large lacA$ Gene  
	Codes for transacetylase,  
		An enzyme adds an [[acetyl group]] ($\ce{CH3CO}$) to lactose.
