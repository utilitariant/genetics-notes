tRNAs consist of folded up RNA strands (74-95 nucleotides long), each unique strand has 2 complementary binding sites.
one that is complementary to an [[mRNA]] [[codon]] and...
another that matches the specific codon with a specific amino acid.

one stem and 3 stem loop structures per tRNA molecule

Amino acids bind to 3' OH hydroxyl group on the stem 

Anti-codon loop contains the anticodon sequence that is complementary to a codon present in mRNA.

Variable loop

as a ribosome reads an mRNA strand codon by codon, it will signal specific tRNA molecules, 
bringing corresponding amino acid monomers to the ribosome, 
where they are held in place and and incorporated into the polymer protein chain 

[[Pre-tRNA]]s are transcribed and processed in the nucleus and then released into the cytoplasm where they are linked to free amino acids for protein synthesis.

structural RNA molecules that were transcribed from genes by RNA polymerase III

 carry a specific amino acid and recognizes one or more of the mRNA codons that define the order of amino acids in a protein.

Aminoacyl-tRNAs bind to the ribosome and add the corresponding amino acid to the polypeptide chain. Therefore, tRNAs are the molecules that actually “translate” the language of RNA into the language of proteins.
each tRNA molecule is linked to its correct amino acid by one of a group of enzymes called [[Aminoacyl tRNA Synthetase]]
